> Status: STORY ASSET / CONTENT EXPERIMENT INPUT

> Boundary: This is not OS policy, not a factual advertising claim set, and not a publish-ready script.

## 0. Experiment objective

Gentlepapa should not be presented as “a company that does many things.” The content hypothesis is that viewers will care only when the founder history is converted into **human conflict, choice, sacrifice, failure, and reversal**.

The goal of this story bank is therefore to preserve only story moments that can later become:

- 15–60 sec vertical short-form drama
- episodic founder-origin series
- brand-film scenes
- AI-generated cinematic sequences using the current Soon Theater production pipeline
- hooks for Reels / Shorts / TikTok / Smart Store social content

Every future episode should be judged by two independent gates:

1. **Audience gate** — would a non-founder viewer stop, continue, and care?
2. **Production gate** — can the current AI video/image pipeline express the scene convincingly within available compute/time?

Recommended verdict vocabulary: `GO / REVISE / KILL`.

---

## 1. Core dramatic thesis

The strongest version of the Gentlepapa story is not:

> “We researched, manufactured, distributed, and sold cosmetics ourselves.”

That is company information, not drama.

The stronger dramatic thesis is:

> A designer who became a developer, suffered from his own scalp problem, entered cosmetics almost by accident, found a raw-material opportunity that had nowhere to go, became a father, abandoned another life plan, borrowed against his newlywed home, and kept rebuilding a shampoo business when production quality and cash flow repeatedly broke down.

The recurring dramatic engine is:

**problem → improbable opportunity → personal reason → risky choice → temporary success → new failure → adaptation**

This is the pattern to preserve across episodes.

## 2. Founder prehistory — story assets

### S01. “The designer who could not understand code”

**Period:** university graduation → early developer training

**Story asset**
- Founder studied visual design.
- After experiencing the early smartphone/iPhone shift, he believed mobile would change the world.
- He entered an IT training program despite having a design background.
- Programming initially felt incomprehensible and overwhelming.

**Conflict:** identity mismatch — designer vs developer.

**Emotional beat:** “I chose the future, but I could not understand its language.”

**Potential hook:**
- “아이폰을 보고 인생을 바꿨는데, 코드는 한 줄도 이해하지 못했다.”

**Production feasibility:** HIGH. AI can easily depict design school, early smartphone fascination, dark classroom, code screens, frustration.

**Audience relevance:** MEDIUM alone; HIGH if used as setup for later founder transformation.

**Verdict:** REVISE as standalone / GO as series opening material.

---

### S02. “Rejected — then calling the interviewer back”

**Period:** first developer job search

**Story asset**
- Founder applied for a developer role despite weak development skills.
- He failed the interview.
- Instead of accepting the rejection, he directly called the interviewer and argued that he could do the job and wanted another chance.
- He was later called back and hired.

**Conflict:** objective qualification says “no”; personal insistence says “one more chance.”

**Turning point:** rejection becomes entry into professional life.

**Potential hook:**
- “면접에서 떨어졌다. 그래서 면접관에게 다시 전화했다.”

**Production feasibility:** VERY HIGH. Phone-call drama is cheap and visually clear.

**Audience relevance:** HIGH — universal rejection/persistence structure.

**Verdict:** GO.

---

### S03. “One year inside a brutal enterprise project”

**Period:** 2011 onward

**Story asset**
- Founder entered a large enterprise tablet development project.
- Roughly a hundred developers were involved.
- Workdays frequently stretched from morning into very late night / early morning.
- This period forced him to learn software and large-project execution under pressure.

**Conflict:** inexperienced newcomer inside a huge professional machine.

**Hidden payoff:** later AI/automation/system-building instincts can be traced to this period.

**Potential hook:**
- “개발을 제대로 모르던 사람이, 수십 명 개발자 사이에서 새벽까지 버텨야 했다.”

**Production feasibility:** HIGH.

**Audience relevance:** MEDIUM unless linked to later scalp stress or current AI-company narrative.

**Verdict:** REVISE.

## 3. The personal problem that later becomes the product reason

### S04. “The shoulders covered in flakes”

**Period:** developer years

**Story asset**
- Severe work stress coincided with persistent scalp discomfort.
- Even with daily shampooing, dandruff/flaking remained visible on his shoulders.
- Conventional mass-market shampoo shopping did not solve the discomfort.
- Scratching the scalp had become habitual.

**Conflict:** daily hygiene ritual fails every day.

**Visual image:** dark clothes / shoulders / flakes / hand repeatedly moving to scalp.

**Potential hook:**
- “매일 머리를 감는데도, 검은 옷을 입을 수가 없었다.”

**Production feasibility:** VERY HIGH.

**Audience relevance:** VERY HIGH for scalp-problem audiences.

**Advertising caution:** keep this as founder experience, not a medical efficacy claim.

**Verdict:** GO — one of the strongest bridge scenes between founder story and product category.

---

### S05. “The sample that stopped the hand”

**Story asset**
- Founder encountered a shampoo sample made with a Houttuynia / Perilla / green-tea related raw-material concept.
- It did not fully solve all flaking concerns, but the scalp felt noticeably more comfortable to him.
- The decisive subjective change was behavioral: his hand stopped going to the scalp as often to scratch.
- That experience triggered the thought: “Maybe this should become a shampoo business.”

**Conflict:** years of unresolved discomfort vs one unexpectedly different usage experience.

**Turning point:** product opportunity becomes personal conviction.

**Potential hook:**
- “샴푸를 바꿨는데, 어느 날 손이 머리로 가지 않았다.”

**Production feasibility:** VERY HIGH.

**Audience relevance:** VERY HIGH.

**Advertising caution:** testimonial framing only; future scripts require claims review.

**Verdict:** GO — S-tier origin moment candidate.

## 4. The stranded raw material — external opportunity

### S06. “A raw material with nowhere to go”

**Story asset**
- An older female business owner had developed an herbal/raw-material concept originally motivated by family hair concerns.
- Investment had been raised for commercialization, but the planned downstream product route failed.
- A large amount of produced raw material remained without a viable sales path.
- The founder later encountered the request to help sell/use this material.

**Conflict:** money has already been spent, material already exists, but there is no functioning product/business route.

**Dramatic value:** this is the “object enters the story” moment — an unwanted asset becomes the seed of Gentlepapa.

**Potential hook:**
- “팔 곳 없는 원료가 남았다. 그게 우리 샴푸의 시작이 됐다.”

**Production feasibility:** HIGH. Tanks, boxes, stored raw material, phone/meeting scenes are easy to stage with AI.

**Audience relevance:** HIGH if simplified. Avoid overloading viewers with investor/family backstory.

**Verdict:** GO with compression.

## 5. Marriage, Europe, and the life-plan reversal

### S07. “We went to Europe to leave Korea”

**Period:** 2016

**Story asset**
- Founder had married and moved his home base toward Ulsan while older business activity still remained connected to Busan.
- The couple spent roughly two months in Europe.
- Germany was explored as a possible base for a cross-border e-commerce / warehouse business and potential relocation.

**Conflict:** the next life was supposed to be overseas, not a Korean shampoo factory/business.

**Potential hook:**
- “젠틀파파를 만들 생각은 없었다. 우리는 독일로 이주할 생각이었다.”

**Production feasibility:** HIGH, but must avoid expensive photorealistic continuity demands; stylized travel montage is preferable.

**Audience relevance:** HIGH because it creates a strong counterfactual path.

**Verdict:** GO.

---

### S08. “The phone call in Europe”

**Story asset**
- During the Europe trip, founder discussed future business directions with an acquaintance/future partner.
- The partner had a chemical-engineering background and could support the cosmetics responsible-sales qualification requirements at the time.
- They compared overseas purchasing/reselling vs creating an OEM cosmetic product.
- The idea of building a shampoo business together began to solidify remotely while the founder was traveling.

**Conflict:** two uncertain business paths; limited resources; qualification constraint.

**Production feasibility:** HIGH.

**Audience relevance:** MEDIUM. Better as connective scene than standalone episode.

**Verdict:** REVISE.

---

### S09. “The pregnancy that changed the destination”

**Story asset**
- Near the end of the Europe trip, founder’s wife felt unwell and spent much of the final stay resting.
- After returning to Korea, they confirmed the pregnancy of their first child.
- The pregnancy disrupted the earlier plan to relocate abroad.
- Both founders/partners were fathers or becoming fathers; this influenced the family-oriented brand direction.

**Conflict:** planned life abroad vs new responsibility and new family reality.

**Turning point:** the business is no longer merely a commercial opportunity; “father” becomes part of the identity.

**Potential hook:**
- “독일로 떠나려던 계획은, 임신 테스트기 두 줄로 끝났다.”

**Production feasibility:** VERY HIGH.

**Audience relevance:** VERY HIGH — human, visual, immediate.

**Verdict:** GO — S-tier episode candidate.

---

### S10. “Gentlepapa becomes the name”

**Story asset**
- The founding group was now strongly oriented around being fathers/family.
- The name “Gentlepapa” emerged and became more convincing when the `.com` domain was available.
- The naming moment helped crystallize the brand.

**Conflict:** abstract project needs an identity.

**Production feasibility:** VERY HIGH.

**Audience relevance:** MEDIUM as standalone; HIGH as payoff at the end of S09.

**Verdict:** GO as ending beat, not full episode.

## 6. Product differentiation — why not just another “natural shampoo”

### S11. “Natural shampoo was gentle — but that was not enough”

**Story asset**
- Founder perceived the natural-shampoo category at the time as focused mainly on mildness / naturalness.
- His own remaining scalp/flaking needs pushed development toward adding a more functional usage concept rather than accepting “gentle but basic.”
- The early Gentlepapa concept aimed to combine mild/natural positioning with a more scalp-problem-oriented usage experience.

**Conflict:** category convention vs founder’s unresolved problem.

**Potential hook:**
- “순한 샴푸는 많았다. 그런데 내 두피 문제는 그대로였다.”

**Production feasibility:** HIGH.

**Audience relevance:** HIGH.

**Claims caution:** “first in market” or comparative ingredient/function claims are unverified and must not be published without external evidence.

**Verdict:** GO conceptually / REVISE factual-superiority claims.

## 7. Bootstrapping and risk

### S12. “We started with almost nothing”

**Story asset**
- Two founders agreed to contribute a small initial amount each.
- Office deposit/rent and basic setup immediately consumed much of the available startup capital.
- Product development and raw-material purchasing required more cash than remained.

**Conflict:** a product idea exists before the money needed to make it real.

**Potential hook:**
- “사무실 문을 열고 나니, 제품을 만들 돈이 없었다.”

**Production feasibility:** VERY HIGH.

**Audience relevance:** HIGH.

**Verdict:** GO.

---

### S13. “The newlywed home becomes business capital”

**Story asset**
- Founder used financing secured against the newlywed household/home to create additional business runway.
- The money was treated as capital for the new venture.

**Conflict:** protect family stability vs risk family assets on the company.

**Emotional weight:** extremely high; use carefully and factually.

**Potential hook:**
- “신혼집을 지켜야 했는데, 나는 그 집을 담보로 사업비를 만들었다.”

**Production feasibility:** VERY HIGH.

**Audience relevance:** VERY HIGH.

**Verdict:** GO — S-tier sacrifice scene.

## 8. Handmade production and first market validation

### S14. “The shampoo was made like a witch’s soup”

**Story asset**
- Early manufacturing environment was extremely manual.
- There was little or no conventional mixing/filling automation.
- Shampoo was effectively handled by hand in a highly labor-intensive way.
- Packaging materials were sourced through places such as Seoul’s Bangsan market.

**Conflict:** trying to build a real consumer brand with almost workshop-level production infrastructure.

**Visual power:** manual stirring, buckets, packaging hunt, hand filling, labels, night work.

**Potential hook:**
- “첫 젠틀파파 샴푸는 공장에서 찍혀 나온 제품이 아니었다.”

**Production feasibility:** VERY HIGH — ideal for AI cinematic reconstruction.

**Audience relevance:** VERY HIGH.

**Verdict:** GO — S-tier visual episode.

---

### S15. “A platform PM believes before the market does”

**Story asset**
- Founder approached Kakao Makers with the product and brand story.
- A platform PM responded positively and actively supported the item.
- Gentlepapa began selling through Makers in 2017.

**Conflict:** unknown small brand seeking its first credible distribution door.

**Turning point:** external validation arrives from a gatekeeper.

**Production feasibility:** HIGH.

**Audience relevance:** MEDIUM-HIGH; stronger when immediately followed by sell-out outcome.

**Verdict:** GO as bridge.

---

### S16. “It sold out — twice”

**Story asset**
- Early Makers sales produced two sell-out cycles, approximately one thousand units per cycle according to founder recollection.
- Initial consumer response was strong enough to create hope that the model worked.

**Conflict:** first validation after high personal risk.

**Dramatic function:** false summit — success must be followed by the production-quality collapse in S17.

**Potential hook:**
- “드디어 됐다고 생각했다. 두 번 연속 완판됐다.”

**Production feasibility:** VERY HIGH.

**Audience relevance:** HIGH when immediately reversed.

**Verification:** exact sales quantities/dates should be checked against records before publication.

**Verdict:** GO.

## 9. The first major failure after success

### S17. “The product changed every time”

**Story asset**
- Because early manufacturing was highly manual, batch/product consistency varied too much.
- Some units/batches felt excellent; others showed separation or quality deviation.
- Founder stress increased because market demand existed but repeatable manufacturing quality was not yet secured.

**Conflict:** sales success vs inability to reproduce the same product reliably.

**This is crucial:** without this failure, the origin story becomes self-congratulatory. This scene supplies the necessary collapse after early success.

**Potential hook:**
- “문제는 안 팔리는 게 아니었다. 팔리는데, 매번 같은 제품을 만들 수가 없었다.”

**Production feasibility:** VERY HIGH. Visually show two bottles, separation, founder comparing batches.

**Audience relevance:** VERY HIGH — unexpected reversal.

**Verdict:** GO — S-tier business drama.

---

### S18. “Revenue existed, but nobody could take money home”

**Period:** through early 2018

**Story asset**
- Sales cash was repeatedly retained for the next production run rather than distributed as income.
- One partner eventually found the personal/family financial pressure unsustainable.
- By early 2018 the partnership was under serious strain despite visible marketplace sales.

**Conflict:** revenue ≠ livelihood.

**Potential hook:**
- “제품은 팔렸는데, 우리는 월급을 가져갈 수 없었다.”

**Production feasibility:** VERY HIGH.

**Audience relevance:** VERY HIGH for founders and general audiences because it overturns the assumption that sales equal success.

**Verdict:** GO — strong season-ending cliffhanger.

## 12. Audience test rules for future scripts

Before producing any episode, score 0–2 on each:

1. **0–2 sec Hook:** Can the first sentence/image create a question?
2. **Human conflict:** Is there something to lose?
3. **Specificity:** Is there a concrete object/action rather than abstract business talk?
4. **Reversal:** Does the situation change direction?
5. **Emotional truth:** Would it still matter if the brand logo were removed?
6. **Brand bridge:** Does Gentlepapa emerge naturally rather than as an advertisement interruption?
7. **Visualizability:** Can the current AI pipeline depict it clearly?
8. **Continuity cost:** Can character/location continuity be maintained without excessive generation overhead?
9. **Claims safety:** Are medical / efficacy / “first-ever” / superiority claims avoided or evidence-backed?
10. **Series value:** Does the ending create a reason to watch the next episode?

Interpretation:
- **17–20:** GO
- **12–16:** REVISE
- **0–11:** KILL or merge into another episode

## 13. Current AI production-pipeline fit

The existing Soon Theater work should be treated as a **prototype laboratory for Gentlepapa brand-film production**, not as an unrelated entertainment project.

Current known production base:
- Galaxy Book6 Ultra class system
- RTX 5070 Laptop GPU, 8 GB VRAM
- WSL / CUDA / PyTorch environment
- ComfyUI-based generation workflow
- capability demonstrated through cinematic Soon Theater / Dune-related output

### Best-fit scene types
- close-up emotional portraits
- symbolic product/problem shots
- phone calls / interviews
- home / office interiors
- travel montage
- factory/workshop reenactments
- product consistency contrast shots
- hands, bottles, mixing, packaging, paperwork, night-work atmospheres

### Higher-risk scene types
- long dialogue with perfect lip sync
- many recurring characters in one scene
- exact historical brand/location reconstruction
- complex crowd scenes
- long continuous action
- exact photorealistic recreation of real persons without controlled character references

### Production principle

Prefer **cinematic fragments + narration + sound design + short dialogue** over trying to generate a conventional live-action drama continuously.

The production constraint is not a weakness; it can become the visual grammar of the series.

## 14. What must be verified before public use

Founder recollection is acceptable for story capture, but publication requires evidence checks for factual claims such as:

- exact dates
- exact investment/loan figures
- exact sales quantities and sell-out counts
- exact project budget/team-size claims from prior employment
- specific ingredient/comparative-function statements
- “first in Korea / first in category” claims
- efficacy or scalp-improvement language
- third-party company/person descriptions

Until verified, scripts should use either:
- founder-personal-memory framing, or
- non-quantified language.
