## 1. Core founder thesis

The founder does not define success primarily as becoming famous or exceptional. The central personal objective is to build a stable family, raise the children well, and create a company that can survive without being permanently dependent on the founder.

This has evolved into a company-level vision:

> Build GP Company into an AI-native operating company that can continue functioning even when the founder is absent, then eventually make the operating system/processes themselves part of the company's transferable enterprise value.

The founder sees GentlePapa not only as a scalp-care product brand, but also as the real-world proving ground where manufacturing, customer counseling, marketing, automation, AI orchestration, and business survival are integrated.

## 2. 2018 — product-market response, partner separation, and sudden growth

### Product origin and family context

During the founder's wife's pregnancy, changes in scalp/hair condition became a practical product-development trigger. The early GentlePapa line was organized around:

- GentlePapa natural shampoo for general scalp
- GentlePapa natural shampoo for oily scalp
- Hair tonic

The founder personally used/experienced scalp discomfort and could empathize strongly with customers who had itching, oiliness, and scalp concerns.

### Initial sales channels

The products began selling online, including Kakao Makers.

The business was not yet producing enough cash for normal household salary. Living expenses and operating expenses were supported by borrowed/invested funds while the founders tried to establish the brand.

### Partner separation

The original business partner faced family-level pressure and conflicting expectations about what business path to pursue. Although the partner wanted to continue, the partnership ended around spring 2018.

After the separation, the founder had to continue almost alone while also solving regulatory/responsible-sales issues through outside support.

### 'Bare-ground' entrepreneurship

At this stage the founder had little awareness of public startup programs, youth startup academies, or government support systems. The business was run largely through trial and error:

- raw material purchasing
- product planning
- marketing
- order processing
- customer service
- regulatory administration

### Influencer/media acceleration

Customers who had purchased through Kakao Makers asked an influencer remembered as 'Director Pi' to review GentlePapa. A favorable review generated a large spike in demand.

Orders began arriving at a rate the existing contract/manual production system could not support. At times, customers had to wait weeks for shampoo.

The oily-scalp shampoo was also featured in a beauty-program context remembered by the founder as receiving a 'beauty label' / recommendation-type recognition, which accelerated demand further.

### Strategic turning point: 'I have to manufacture this myself.'

Because production capacity could not keep up with demand, the founder received platform penalties and experienced severe fulfillment pressure.

This created one of the clearest strategic decisions in the founder story:

> If manufacturing is the bottleneck, GP Company itself must become the manufacturer.

This is the origin point of GP Company's move from brand/operator toward direct cosmetics manufacturing.

## 3. Late 2018–2019 — building the factory from almost nothing

### Capital and facility strategy

The founder received approximately KRW 50 million in family investment/support for the manufacturing build-out. The amount was meaningful but not enough to build a conventional cosmetics factory comfortably.

The founder studied cosmetics manufacturing-facility requirements and learned that the facility needed multiple separated spaces/sections.

Instead of paying to build every partition from an empty shell, the founder searched for an existing academy-style space in Guyeong-ri, Ulsan, where rooms were already divided. This became a cost-saving facility-design strategy.

Interior work began around late 2018, and the cosmetics manufacturing license/approval was obtained around March 2019.

### Equipment discovery

At the time, the founder found very little practical information online about what small cosmetics manufacturers should actually buy.

The founder spent months researching equipment and eventually found a company in Yangsan that mainly produced food-industry mixing/filling equipment.

The resulting first core production setup included approximately:

- 130 L mixer
- about 1 HP drive
- temperature control
- high-viscosity filling machine
- fill-stop logic based on target weight/volume settings

The founder participated directly in specifying/developing the filling behavior needed for GentlePapa.

This equipment became the foundation of GP Company's manufacturing line.

### Scale-up failure and learning

Moving a small-batch formulation into a 130 L machine did not work automatically.

Problems included:

- dissolving/thickening raw materials consistently
- fixed/limited agitation power
- temperature-control learning
- clogged outlet geometry
- liquid/product spills in the production room
- repeated disposal of failed batches
- inability to sell some outsourced pre-produced inventory because of separation/instability

The team spent roughly two months repeatedly failing and adjusting the process.

Around May 2019, the company succeeded in producing its first roughly 200-unit large batch.

However, even that first successful-looking batch later showed separation and required recall/redevelopment.

Around mid-2019, the formulation/process was adjusted again and the team finally achieved a stable, non-separating large-batch product.

### Meaning of this period

This was not merely a product-development phase. It taught the founder that:

1. lab/small-batch recipes and factory-scale processes are different systems,
2. manufacturing capability itself can become a competitive advantage,
3. manufacturing errors directly become brand/customer-trust problems,
4. process knowledge needs to be captured as a repeatable system rather than remaining in one person's head.

## 4. Customer service as the original GentlePapa data asset

Around 2018–2019, customers used Kakao's then-new Plus Friend/brand messaging channel to send scalp questions at all hours, including late night and early morning.

There were no AI agents or mature chatbots available to the founder. The founder replied personally because he understood the discomfort from his own scalp experience.

One of the strongest founder memories is:

- caring for a newborn at night,
- waking repeatedly,
- and still answering customer scalp questions in real time.

Importantly, the founder remembers this period not only as exhausting but as deeply joyful. The birth of the first child and the feeling that GentlePapa was genuinely helping people made the brand feel worth passing on to the next generation.

This period can be treated as the origin of GP Company's customer-insight database mindset: repeated real-world questions revealed actual customer needs long before the company had structured CRM/AI systems.

## 5. 2019–2020 — growth expectations, hiring, and the cost of organization

### 2019 constraints

The founder's wife had developed knowledge through natural-cosmetics education/certification and had been an important product-development partner.

A later pregnancy reduced her ability to participate in formulation work, particularly where strong essential oils/raw materials were involved.

As a result, the company entered 2019 with a stable but narrow product portfolio and could not expand new-product development as aggressively as planned.

Kakao Makers continued to provide meaningful sales, including repeated sell-outs according to founder recollection.

### Sales trajectory (founder recollection; verify before external use)

Approximate spoken figures:

- 2017: very small/early-stage revenue
- 2018: about KRW 90 million
- 2019: about KRW 120 million
- 2020 onward: revenue declined rather than reaching the expected next growth stage

### 2020 plan

At the start of 2020, the founder expected continued growth and aimed for approximately KRW 200 million revenue.

The company hired additional staff, including:

- production staff
- junior marketing staff
- junior developer/computer-science graduate

The founder wanted to build systems instead of running the company by ad hoc manual work. This revived the founder's earlier developer identity and became an early precursor to the later Workbench concept.

### COVID shock and organizational mismatch

COVID disrupted the expected trajectory.

At the same time, inexperienced junior employees required significant training and management. Payroll, four major insurances, materials, advertising, and operating costs consumed borrowed funds quickly.

The founder borrowed additional operating capital through public/small-business or SME channels.

Marketing experiments included channels such as:

- blog
- Instagram
- influencer/product seeding
- Smart Store keyword/power-link advertising
- Google display-type advertising

Many of these attempts did not produce a repeatable growth engine.

The marketing hire left during 2020, and the developer hire also did not become a sustainable long-term solution.

This period reinforced another long-term lesson:

> Hiring people is not the same as building a system. Without a clear operating system, more people can increase coordination cost faster than output.

## 6. 2021–2023 — contraction, burnout, family pressure, and survival mode

Revenue continued to decline, with the founder recalling roughly KRW 60 million-level annual revenue around 2021 (verify before external use).

The company returned to a much smaller operating structure: the founder plus a production employee for a period, with the founder again responsible for marketing and broader management.

Borrowed funds were increasingly consumed by:

- payroll
- debt repayment
- raw materials
- advertising
- packaging/container purchasing during COVID-era supply constraints

By 2022 the founder describes entering a clear burnout/survival period.

Household spending became extremely constrained. The founder also experienced serious credit/debt-management difficulties.

Family medical/care events involving a child created repeated periods when one or both parents had to stop normal business activity and focus on hospital/care responsibilities. Exact medical details are intentionally omitted from this orchestration context.

The founder describes parts of the COVID/post-COVID period as difficult to remember clearly because business, debt, childcare, illness/injury, and operational stress overlapped.

By 2023, GentlePapa was substantially weakened:

- little marketing budget
- difficulty sustaining content production
- founder mental/physical exhaustion
- intermittent attempts to restart once childcare conditions improved

A key continuation point for the next founder-story session is the founder's first meaningful export/trade-mission attempt through KOTRA in late 2023, undertaken while family responsibilities were still heavy.

## 7. 2026 current state — not 'finished', but transformed

As of 2026-08-11, the founder does not describe the business as financially secure. Cash remains tight and legacy borrowing still affects household/business decisions.

However, the business has changed structurally in important ways.

### Current operating assets

The founder now has:

- direct cosmetics manufacturing capability
- years of scalp-product formulation/manufacturing experience
- direct customer-counseling history
- founder-led design/marketing capability
- Smart Store sales infrastructure
- AI Workbench concept and implementation work
- API-connected commerce/automation experiments
- local Mac mini/server infrastructure
- Hermes/orchestration experiments
- active use of multiple frontier AI coding/assistant systems

The founder's wife is also using AI to reduce fear/friction in cosmetics development and improve development capability.

### AI-native company thesis

GP Company is intentionally being rebuilt as an AI-native company.

The goal is not merely 'use AI a lot.'

The goal is:

1. encode work into repeatable systems,
2. reduce founder-only dependency,
3. let AI agents execute bounded work reliably,
4. make business operations observable and transferable,
5. prove the operating model through GentlePapa commerce/manufacturing,
6. eventually make the company attractive as an acquisition/transferable operating asset.

This is a direct continuation of lessons learned from the 2018–2021 period: capacity bottlenecks, founder overload, inexperienced hiring, ad hoc marketing, and knowledge trapped in individuals.

## 8. Immediate commercial bottleneck: Smart Store sales

The founder considers Smart Store revenue growth the most important near-term proving ground.

Current reasons include:

- fast settlement/cash conversion
- current limitations in the self-hosted mall's card-payment/PG flow due to credit-related constraints
- customer leakage when checkout becomes difficult
- mismatch between self-mall loyalty/grade/point structures and Smart Store
- changes in Naver shopping/store discovery that make exposure more difficult than in earlier years

Therefore the immediate problem should not be framed as 'make more AI content.'

A better operational framing is:

> Build a measurable Smart Store acquisition → product-page conversion → purchase → repurchase system, and use AI to improve each step.

This should become a contained orchestration program rather than dozens of disconnected AI tasks.

Suggested first experiment object:

**One flagship oily-scalp shampoo Smart Store conversion project**

Possible workstream:

- search/discovery hypothesis
- listing/title/keyword structure
- thumbnail/visual creative
- founder-story trust elements
- product-page restructuring
- review/FAQ/customer-question mining
- conversion measurement
- repurchase bridge
- weekly experiment log

Do one SKU end-to-end first; generalize only after measurable improvement.

## 9. Founder-story content pillars extracted from this session

These are reusable narrative assets for GentlePapa/GP Company content.

### Pillar A — 'Demand arrived before the factory existed'
A favorable external review caused orders to explode, but production could not keep up. Platform penalties and customer waits led to the decision to become a manufacturer.

### Pillar B — 'The factory built inside an academy layout'
With limited capital, the founder used an already-partitioned academy space to satisfy manufacturing-space needs more economically.

### Pillar C — 'The first 130 L batch failed'
Large-scale manufacturing required repeated failures, spills, disposal, recalls, and process redesign before a stable product emerged.

### Pillar D — 'Customer counseling at 3 a.m. while raising a baby'
The founder answered scalp questions personally while caring for a newborn, because he understood the customers' discomfort from experience.

### Pillar E — 'Hiring did not create a system'
Marketing and developer hires during the growth/COVID period increased coordination and cash burn without producing a durable operating engine.

### Pillar F — 'Why Workbench exists'
Workbench/AI orchestration is not a technology hobby. It is the founder's answer to years of founder overload, fragmented knowledge, expensive coordination, and repeated operational dependency.

### Pillar G — 'The company is still in the story'
The narrative should not pretend the founder already 'won.' As of 2026 the business remains financially constrained, but manufacturing knowledge, AI systems, and operating insight are far stronger than during the original growth period.

This honesty is a core trust asset.

## 10. Narrative tone rules for future content

When turning this history into videos/posts/pages:

- Do not convert it into a generic heroic success story.
- Do not present unresolved financial hardship as already overcome.
- Avoid pity-focused storytelling.
- Emphasize decisions, experiments, mistakes, learning, and system-building.
- Family is the founder's reason for continuing, not a device for emotional manipulation.
- Customer pain should be described with empathy but without medical claims.
- Separate verified dates/facts from founder recollection.
- Use concrete scenes: order alerts, delayed shipments, factory floor spills, 130 L mixer, late-night customer messages, small team payroll pressure, restarting after setbacks.
